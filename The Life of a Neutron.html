⚛️ The Life of a Neutron

1 · Birth: a neutron is born in fission

  • A slow (thermal) neutron (0.025 eV, ~2.2 km/s) is absorbed by U-235 → unstable U-236*.
  • It splits into two fragments and ~2.4 prompt neutrons on average, plus ~200 MeV of energy.
  • Those neutrons are born fast: ~2 MeV on average, ~20,000 km/s (≈6% of light speed), roughly 10 million times the energy of a thermal neutron.

2 · The problem: a sea of U-238

In 5% enriched fuel, 95 of every 100 uranium atoms are U-238 (each 1 dot = 1 atom; red = U-235).

  • U-238 only fissions above ~1 MeV, and even then rarely.
  • Fast neutrons hit U-235 with tiny odds (σ ≈ 1 barn).
  • Heavy nuclei barely slow a neutron: an elastic hit on U-238 costs under 1% of its energy.

Light nuclei act like billiard balls of equal mass: hydrogen can take nearly all the energy in one hit. That is why reactors surround fuel with water.

6 · The life, in short

  • Born fast (~2 MeV) from U-235 fission.
  • Danger zone: U-238 is ~95% of the fuel. It scatters fast neutrons, but its resonances (1 eV–10 keV) can swallow them.
  • Moderator (water) slows the neutron in ~20 collisions, quickly crossing the resonances.
  • Thermal: U-235’s fission cross section (~585 b) dwarfs U-238’s capture (~2.7 b), so even at 5% the neutron most often causes another fission.
  • If captured by U-238 it isn’t wasted: it becomes Pu-239, which also fissions.
